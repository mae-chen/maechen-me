Worm Gear Tensioning

Higher Ground · TrackPod

TrackPod pointing accuracy is sensitive to backlash in its elevation and skew axes. Worm gears used to be tensioned by hand (tighten a set screw until you feel resistance), which varied from operator to operator. I developed an automated, closed-loop system that reads StallGuard motor-load data to find the right tensioning endpoint. It combines two motorized gantries, an electrical interface to the TrackPod, stepper controllers, and a Raspberry Pi that guides the operator and logs the data.

Sun Loading Experiments

Higher Ground · TrackPod

I ran controlled experiments to characterize how solar irradiance drives TrackPod heat gain, feeding the product’s heat dashboard. That meant building a repeatable test setup, improving temperature and irradiance measurements, and refining test articles to better represent the TrackPod. Interchangeable light-filtering covers created repeatable lower-irradiance conditions, producing data that links irradiance to internal temperature rise.

Propulsion (Wheel) Motors

NASA Johnson Space Center · Lunar Terrain Vehicle Ground Test Unit (LTV-GTU)

Each of the rover’s four legs has independent suspension and steering, with two motors and two tires per leg. Many stators had not been clamped to the required 130 ft·lbs and slipped inside the housing, the root cause of problems that had lingered for months. With a deadline looming, my team and I rebuilt two motors in under three days. I also rebuilt a brake disk assembly after a broken caliper wire melted a brake spacer, and designed a small 3D-printed piece to hold the spring pins that keep the motor together.

Custom Milled Tools

NASA Johnson Space Center · Lunar Terrain Vehicle Ground Test Unit (LTV-GTU)

Several motor casings had stuck resolver rings at the bottom of a deep bowl, removable only with a tool that mates to the ring without damaging the casing or threads. I learned to write CAM programs in Fusion and run a 3-axis Haas CNC mill, then made five tight-tolerance aluminum tools, each fitted to its component and driven by a ratchet wrench, tuning machine parameters to balance speed, tool health, and accuracy.

Test Driver Comfort

NASA Johnson Space Center · Lunar Terrain Vehicle Ground Test Unit (LTV-GTU)

Long-range test runs exposed three issues: drivers sat in the Texas sun for hours, the edgekey display was too dim to read, and there was nowhere to secure water bottles. A retractable sunshade, mounted using existing accessory-bar holes to stay minimally invasive, solved the first two, and printed bottle holders solved the third. A second version will add a deployable arm for more shade.

Goldfish: Arm and End Effector

Golden Warriors Robotics FRC #8576 · Goldfish (2025 robot)

For 2025 I challenged my team to build something unique. My game analysis showed a mid-tier team could win by filling a niche, so I designed intake, arm, and shooter subsystems to launch 16" balls into a net 8 ft up, in an 8-week build with very limited mentorship. I served as project manager, designer, drive coach, and robot operator on a student-led team.

The arm rotates over the shooter to hand off balls, and with just a pivot and one axis of wheels (two degrees of freedom) it picks up from the ground and two elevated heights. No other team had this arm and shooter combination, so the concept was unproven until our first prototype. Computer vision lines up shots, the shooter varies speed with distance, macros sequence the subsystems, and encoders with PID control the arm and intake. The design won the Texas State Championship Creativity Award.
